## Reason for referral

The patient was referred by her general practitioner with six months of fatigue, feeling cold, constipation, dry skin and a weight gain of 5 kg. Her GP had found a raised thyroid-stimulating hormone on two occasions and asked for advice on diagnosis and treatment.

## History

She works as a primary school teacher. There is no history of neck surgery, radiotherapy or recent pregnancy. Her mother has hypothyroidism. She takes no regular medicines and does not smoke.

She has a severe allergy to sulfonamide antibiotics: in 2021 she developed facial swelling (angioedema) and hives within hours of taking sulfamethoxazole for a urinary infection and required emergency treatment. This allergy is recorded as severe.

## Examination

Pulse 58 regular, blood pressure 118/76 mmHg, weight 72 kg. The thyroid gland was diffusely, mildly enlarged and non-tender, with no discrete nodules. Reflexes showed slow relaxation. No signs of other endocrine disease.

## Investigations

Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) measured in clinic today was 8.6 mIU/L. Thyroid peroxidase antibodies were reported as strongly positive by the GP laboratory last month.

## Diagnosis

Primary hypothyroidism (ICD-10 E03.9), most likely autoimmune (Hashimoto's thyroiditis).

## Plan

Dr. Halvorsen started levothyroxine 50 micrograms once daily, to be taken on an empty stomach 30 minutes before breakfast.

Grace Adeyemi explained how to take the tablets, the need to separate them from iron or calcium supplements by four hours, and the symptoms of over-replacement. Written information was provided.

The following steps were agreed:

1. Repeat TSH in six to eight weeks via the GP and adjust the dose in steps of 25 micrograms.
2. Target TSH within the reference range, ideally 0.5 to 2.5 mIU/L.
3. Patient to inform us if she plans a pregnancy, because the dose usually needs to be increased early.
4. Sulfonamide allergy to be flagged on all prescribing systems.

We have not arranged a further hospital appointment; we are happy to see her again if TSH remains above the target range after two dose adjustments.
